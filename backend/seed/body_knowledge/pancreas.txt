The pancreas sits behind the stomach and has two important jobs.

One job is digestive: it makes enzymes that help break down carbs, proteins, and fats in the small intestine.

Those enzymes travel through the pancreatic duct into the duodenum, the first part of the small intestine.

The other job is hormonal: tiny islet cells release insulin and glucagon into the blood.

Insulin helps cells take in sugar for energy. Glucagon helps raise blood sugar when it gets too low.

Only a small part of the pancreas makes hormones; most of it makes digestive juice.

Eating balanced meals and staying active help the pancreas keep blood sugar steadier.
